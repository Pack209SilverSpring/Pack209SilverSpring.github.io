## Cub Scout Family Basics
{: class="mt-12 text-2xl font-bold text-forest" }

As a Cub Scout parent/caregiver, your involvement is integral to your Scout having a great experience — one of the great joys of Scouting is the family-centered aspect of the program. Here's what you can do to help your Cub Scout succeed:

- Get your Cub Scout to events and programs on time and prepared — put Den and Pack events on your calendar. Find the Pack [calendar here](/calendar).
- Ensure your Cub Scout has a uniform and gear.
- Help your Cub Scout complete their Adventure/Rank requirements, decide what other recognition they'd like to earn, and track/report requirements to the Pack or Den. Make sure they finish any "home assignments."
- Offer to help out! The Pack and Dens need parent help to make events and activities happen — see Help Wanted below.
- Ensure your Cub Scout follows Pack rules and everyone is enjoying themselves safely — see Cub Behavior below.
- Have fun! Take part and enjoy spending time with your Cub Scout and their friends.
{: class="mt-4 space-y-3 leading-7 list-disc list-outside pl-5" }

[Click here to access Cub Scouting information from Scouting America](https://www.scouting.org/).

## Help Wanted: How to "Help the Pack Go!"
{: class="mt-12 text-2xl font-bold text-forest" }

Cub Scouts is a volunteer organization and it can't happen without the help of many parents/caregivers in many different ways. Every family is expected to help the Den and/or Pack in some capacity — special talent, relationship, or job, all effort is helpful. Even if you don't feel you bring something "special," just the effort and help you can give adds to the program — and you'll get far more back than you contribute.

In addition to Den Leaders/Assistant Den Leaders, the Pack relies on volunteers for: Committee Chair, Cubmaster/Assistant Cubmaster(s), Treasurer, New Membership Coordinator, Pack Trainer, Campout Coordinator, Hikemaster, Pinewood Derby Lead, leads for other yearly Pack activities, and a Webmaster. Some positions are filled for this year, but some are open — please consider volunteering, or ask any current leader for more information.

## Cub Behavior
{: class="mt-12 text-2xl font-bold text-forest" }

As a parent/caregiver, you're our partner in ensuring Cub Scouts follow the rules and behave appropriately so everyone is safe and can have fun.

<div class="grid gap-6 mt-4 sm:grid-cols-2" markdown="1">
<div markdown="1">

### Scouts are expected to:
{: class="font-bold text-cub-blue" }

- Have fun, and do their best
- Get dirty on campouts
- Spend enjoyable time with friends and family
- Follow the rules and directions of Leaders and parents/caregivers
- Uphold the spirit of the Scout Oath and Scout Law
- Behave appropriately for the setting — running outside at a campout is okay; running by the campfire or in the church hall is not
{: class="mt-2 space-y-1 list-disc list-outside pl-5 text-slate-700" }

</div>
<div markdown="1">

### Scouts are expected NOT to:
{: class="font-bold text-cub-blue" }

- Bully
- Tease
- Ignore Leader or parent/caregiver instructions
- Yell and run around in the church hall
{: class="mt-2 space-y-1 list-disc list-outside pl-5 text-slate-700" }

</div>
</div>

Parents/caregivers are expected to help ensure all Scouts follow these simple rules, so everyone can have fun safely and we respect our host, Silver Spring United Methodist Church.

## Health & Safety
{: class="mt-12 text-2xl font-bold text-forest" }

- Please notify Pack and Den leadership of any medical requirements or allergies. Annual health/medical forms are required for campouts.
- Youth Protection Training is required of all adult leaders, and Pack events follow BSA's "two-deep" leadership rule &mdash; at least two trained adults present at every activity.
- Several rank Adventures are personal-safety themed, so Scouts build these skills as part of the regular program.
- Please follow site-specific guidelines at campouts and events, and be courteous of other families' needs and comfort levels.
{: class="mt-4 space-y-3 leading-7 list-disc list-outside pl-5" }

Keeping kids safe guides everything Scouting America does &mdash; as they put it, <em>"Being a leader in Scouting America is a privilege, not a right."</em> Every adult leader completes youth protection training before working with Scouts and renews it annually, and background checks and BSA's "two-deep" leadership policy add further safeguards. Read more, including how to report a concern, on Scouting America's [Youth Protection page](https://www.scouting.org/training/safeguarding-youth/).

## Uniforms & Where to Buy
{: class="mt-12 text-2xl font-bold text-forest" }

Cub Scouts wear two uniforms. <strong>"Class A"</strong> is the traditional blue Cub Scout shirt with neckerchief, slide, and hat specific to the Scout's level — the belt displays earned "belt loop" awards. Official pants/socks are optional; dark pants or clean jeans work fine. The blue shirt purchased in a Scout's first year can be worn through 5th grade, so size up. Gently used hand-me-downs from prior Pack families may be available in our rummage bin. <strong>"Class B"</strong> is our pack t-shirt, whose cost is included in annual dues. [Read more about Cub Scout uniforms](https://www.scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/cub-scout-uniform/).

Any family struggling with the cost of a uniform, Scouts BSA registration, or Pack dues should speak privately with the Cubmaster — assistance may be available.
